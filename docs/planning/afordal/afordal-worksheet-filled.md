## 2. The ask

What you walked into:
> A stakeholder had a rough idea and a set of wireframes that were three years old and never finished. No user flows, no content strategy, no design system.

What you didn't have:
> No spec, no research, no existing patterns, no stakeholder clarity on who the product was really for.

---

## 3. The real problem

Different people it had to work for:
> Buyers, sellers, agents, loan officers — each crossing paths, each arriving under different circumstances.

Real-world rules/constraints:
> IDX and MLS compliance rules limiting how listing data could be displayed and attributed outside the source system.

What made it genuinely hard:
> Untangling who the product served and what rules it had to obey before any screen could be designed.

---

## 4. Working it out — decision 1

Problem:
> Buyers could arrive three different ways: invited by an agent, invited by a loan officer, or with no agent at all. One onboarding flow didn't fit all three.

First attempt:
> A single linear onboarding form, same questions for everyone.

Final direction:
> Branching onboarding that adapts based on entry point — agent-invited buyers skip agent-matching, buyers with no agent get that step built in.

Why:
> Kept one system instead of three separate products, while respecting that people arrived in genuinely different situations.

Have both versions to show?
> Yes — in Figma files.

---

## 4. Working it out — decision 2

Problem:
> Wanted to show rich listing detail directly in the buyer/seller dashboard, but IDX/MLS rules restrict how listing data can be displayed and attributed outside the source system.

First attempt:
> Full listing detail shown inline.

Final direction:
> Compliant summary view with a clear path to the full listing.

Why:
> The constraint shaped the information architecture — the dashboard summarizes and routes rather than duplicates, which kept it compliant and actually reduced clutter.

Have both versions to show?
> Yes — in Figma files.

---

## 4. Working it out — decision 3

Problem:
> Special financing options were added late as a differentiator, meant to expose buyers/sellers to programs normally explained only by an agent or loan officer.

First attempt:
> A financing CTA placed only at the final financing step.

Final direction:
> Financing education woven earlier into the decision path, not tacked on at the end.

Why:
> The goal was to help people make better decisions earlier, not just mention financing at the last moment — financing education became part of the core journey.

Have both versions to show?
> Yes — in Figma files.

## 5. The system

> Built a full design system and brand asset library from scratch, since none existed. This system extended into the CRM and marketing site as well, not just the original app.

---

## 6. The ecosystem

Four connected surfaces, one design system:
> 1. Buyer/seller app
> 2. CRM for agents and loan officers to manage their pipeline
> 3. Marketing site to bring agents/loan officers onto the platform
> 4. Shareable IDX home search agents can send to contacts (reskin of an existing HomeASAP tool — worth being upfront about, not a from-scratch design problem, but shows reach of the system)

---

## 7. Outcome

> Shipped solo in six months, live today as part of the Afordal/RatePlug/HomeASAP product. Separately, that same UI foundation was reused as the basis for a commercial real estate SaaS product through a joint venture, a downstream reuse beyond the original consumer-facing platform.
