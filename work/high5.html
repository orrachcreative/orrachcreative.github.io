The ask

High5, a consulting talent marketplace connecting independent talent with 1,600+ companies worldwide, needed two things that don't usually come from the same person: someone to build the brand from the ground up, and someone the CMO could hand the whole creative operation to. I was brought on as both — the single point of contact for a design team of 4–5 people spread across different countries, responsible for creative direction, timelines, and delivery.

There was no existing brand system to inherit, and no separation between the person making creative calls and the person managing the client relationship — every design decision was also, immediately, an account-management decision.

The real problem

The brand had to hold together across an unusually wide range of output: a corporate website, ad campaigns, social content, video storyboards handed off to animators, internal communications, and large-format signage for trade-show conventions — all produced by a team that never worked in the same room or timezone.

The harder constraint wasn't any single deliverable, it was consistency without proximity: keeping a distributed team's output looking like one brand, and keeping the CMO's trust, at the same time.

The system

The brand reference from Decision 1 became the backbone for the corporate site (built with a dev team), ad campaigns, social content, video storyboards, internal comms, and convention signage — one visual language instead of five separately-branded deliverables.
