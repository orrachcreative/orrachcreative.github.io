The ask

High5 needed two things at once, and most people only apply for one of them: someone to art-direct a real estate CRM toward a coherent visual and interaction language, and someone the client trusted enough to manage the relationship directly. I was hired into both roles at the same time.

There was no design system to inherit and no single source of truth for what “on brand” meant yet — and no separation between the person making design calls and the person managing client expectations, which meant every design decision was also, immediately, an account-management decision.

The real problem

High5 served four distinct people through one CRM: buyers, sellers, agents, and loan officers, each with a different relationship to the platform and a different definition of what the tool was for.

The harder constraint wasn’t any one of those user types, though — it was that the client's ambitions moved faster than a design team could safely absorb without either the timeline or the design quality quietly breaking. Holding the account relationship and the design direction in the same hands meant I couldn’t let one job's pressure quietly compromise the other.

The system

The component system built in Decision 1 became the shared reference for every designer on the account: type and color rules, core components, and the patterns that kept the CRM's buyer, seller, agent, and loan-officer views feeling like one product instead of four.
