The ask

HomeASAP wasn’t a blank slate — it was already a decade into serving real estate agents and brokerages with branded marketing websites and IDX search when I joined as Lead Visual Designer. The ask wasn’t “build this,” it was “modernize this without anyone noticing the ground moved.”

There was no clean spec for that, because the constraint wasn’t creative, it was operational: thousands of live, agent-branded sites already running on the existing visual system, each one somebody's actual business.

The real problem

Two very different audiences depended on the same platform: the agents and brokerages who were HomeASAP’s paying customers, and the buyers, sellers, and loan officers who used those agents’ sites without ever knowing HomeASAP’s name. IDX and MLS compliance rules constrained what listing data could be shown and how, on every one of those sites at once.

The genuinely hard part was sequencing: any visual or system change had to roll out gradually across a decade of live client sites without a single one visibly breaking mid-transition, which ruled out most of the fast, direct approaches that would have worked on a greenfield product.

The system

The brand-token theme system from Decision 3 became the backbone for every new agent site: shared structural components and interaction patterns, with each agent's logo, color, and imagery layered on top without touching the underlying UX.

The ecosystem

HomeASAP wasn’t one website, it was a platform generating many: an agent-branded site template used at scale, and the shared IDX search tool underneath all of them.
