The ask

I joined HomeASAP in 2013 when it was still small, as a Senior Visual Designer focused on marketing. There was no formal product-design function yet — the job was to contribute wherever the growing company needed design: branding, marketing, Facebook advertising, and the MLS/IDX systems real estate agents relied on for lead generation and lead nurture.

There was no single "ask" so much as a company that kept getting bigger around me, for thirteen years, and a role that had to grow with it.

The real problem

As the product lineup expanded into IDX Home Search, Page Engage, Home Value Leads, TurnKey Suite, and more — tools that now account for the majority of the company's revenue — visual/marketing design skill alone wasn't enough. The job had to evolve into real product design: research, systems, and UX for SaaS tools serving real estate agents and brokerages directly, not just the marketing that brought them in the door.

The hard part wasn't any single product, it was the transition itself — becoming a product designer while still owning the marketing and lead-generation work that had always been the job, without a clean handoff point where one ended and the other began.

The system

The product-design practice from Decision 1 became the shared foundation across IDX Home Search, Page Engage, Home Value Leads, and TurnKey Suite — the tools that now account for the majority of HomeASAP's revenue — plus the landing-page system from Decision 2 running the corporate site.

Thirteen years and counting — from a Senior Visual Designer hired when the company was small, to the Principal Product Designer owning design across the product suite that drives the majority of its revenue today.
